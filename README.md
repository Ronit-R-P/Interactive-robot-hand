# TruTouch — An Open-Source, Modular Prosthetic Hand

**TruTouch** is a low-cost, customizable, 3D-printed prosthetic hand developed to explore accessible assistive-technology design. The project focuses on a modular mechanical structure that can be reproduced, assembled, and adapted using accessible manufacturing methods.

The current version is intended primarily as a **demonstration and preliminary testing platform**, allowing prospective users to explore basic prosthetic-hand movement before considering a more expensive, permanent prosthetic solution.

##Visualize: https://ronit-r-p.github.io/Interactive-robot-hand/

## Project Overview

Access to prosthetic devices can involve a substantial financial commitment. TruTouch explores an alternative approach: an openly documented, customizable robotic hand that can be fabricated using 3D printing and commonly available electronic and mechanical components.

The design emphasizes affordability, modularity, and reproducibility. Its mechanical components are designed around specified dimensions and tolerances, with the aim of supporting fabrication using different 3D-printing materials and adapting the design to different requirements.

An accompanying interactive 3D model allows users to rotate the hand, inspect its structure, and observe finger movements. This provides a visual demonstration of the design and its articulation.

## Key Features

- **Low-Cost Design:** Explores an affordable approach to developing a prosthetic-hand demonstration platform.
- **Modular Construction:** Organizes the mechanical design into components that can be assembled, modified, or replaced.
- **3D-Printable Components:** Uses additive manufacturing to support local fabrication and design iteration.
- **Material Flexibility:** Aims to accommodate different 3D-printing materials while maintaining dimensional compatibility through specified tolerances and appropriate print calibration.
- **Articulated Fingers:** Incorporates mechanical finger joints and a tendon-driven mechanism to produce finger movement.
- **Electronic Actuation:** Uses an electronic control system to actuate the hand's fingers.
- **Interactive 3D Demonstration:** Provides a digital model that users can rotate and interact with to visualize the hand's movement.
- **Open-Source Replication:** Intended to make the design and documentation available for others to reproduce, evaluate, and improve.

## Mechanical Design and Manufacturing

The hand is designed using computer-aided design (CAD) and manufactured through 3D printing.

The mechanical assembly incorporates articulated finger segments, a palm structure, tendon-routing features, and provisions for mounting actuation components. The design supports iterative refinement of dimensions, joints, assembly clearances, and component interfaces.

An important design objective is to make the model reproducible across different printing materials. Components are designed with specified dimensions and tolerances so that compatible parts can be fabricated without redesigning the entire assembly for every material. Material-dependent shrinkage, warping, strength, and print settings may still require calibration and validation.

The modular approach is intended to simplify maintenance, customization, and future design improvements.

## Electronic and Control System

The prototype combines mechanical actuation with an electronic control system to demonstrate finger movement.

The electronics and actuation architecture remain open to further development. Motor selection, power supply, battery placement, packaging, and control mechanisms may be revised as the design evolves.

The current project therefore focuses on demonstrating the mechanical concept and its basic movement rather than claiming a finalized, production-ready electronic architecture.

## Interactive 3D Model

An accompanying interactive visualization allows users to explore the hand digitally.

Users can rotate the model, examine its geometry from different viewpoints, and interact with available finger movements to understand the hand's articulation.

This feature complements the physical design by making the mechanism easier to demonstrate and understand without requiring users to handle the physical prototype.

## Intended Applications

TruTouch is currently being developed as a demonstration and preliminary testing platform.

Potential applications include:

- Demonstrating basic prosthetic-hand movement to prospective users.
- Supporting demonstrations in hospitals and rehabilitation centres, subject to appropriate safety review.
- Exploring the mechanical design and operation of an articulated hand.
- Providing a starting point for students, researchers, and developers working on assistive technologies.
- Enabling collaborative improvements through shared design files and documentation.

The long-term vision may include further development toward a wearable prosthetic device. However, the current prototype is not presented as a clinically validated or ready-to-use medical prosthesis.

## Safety and Limitations

TruTouch is an evolving engineering prototype intended for demonstration and preliminary evaluation.

Its structural strength, material suitability, actuation reliability, electrical safety, battery integration, ergonomics, and suitability for human wear require further evaluation. The design should not be treated as a medically approved prosthesis or used as a substitute for professional prosthetic assessment.

Any future wearable application would require appropriate engineering validation, safety testing, and assessment with qualified professionals and intended users.

## Future Development
